The District Court’s January 31, 2024 merits order (DC Dkt. 396), June 15, 2026 fee order (DC Dkt. 423), and June 23, 2026 final judgment of $211,747.77 (DC Dkt. 427) are under review in CAAP-26-0000625. The letter states certified trial-court and agency record facts.

Day 0 — October 31, 2017

After a failed June 28, 2017 five-year safety test on Elevator #2 and DLIR Invoice #80649 (September 7, 2017, Bates AOAO 0328), operating permits expire for HAW #67-127 and HAW #67-120 (DC Dkt. 287, Ex. 17, AOAO 0326–0328). The letter describes 232 condominium units.

Day 190 — May 9–10, 2018

Basement hoistway doors on Elevator #1 decouple. Stuart Masami Uchida re-hangs the doors with $0.00 in parts, notes a missing or broken overhead J-track stop bolt, and records no “misuse” (Trial Ex. J-14, AOAO 0867–0869; omitted page 4, AOAO 0870; October 26, 2022 trial transcript, DC Dkts. 348–349). On May 16 an off-site manager adds a $2,260.15 “misuse” label (Trial Ex. J-20).

Days 328–349 — September 24–October 15, 2018

Requests for permit numbers, certifications, contracts, and door-incident logs (PIKOR000279–280; Trial Ex. J-9). The letter states counsel produced zero permits, required a notarized affidavit and advance fees, and confirmed zero investigative minutes (Trial Ex. J-10). HIOSH reports “no such records” of HAR § 12-229-10.1 owner accident reports (Trial Ex. J-29).

Day 351 — October 17, 2018

A Unit #506 mobility scooter decouples the same Elevator #1 doors ($1,045.02, Trial Ex. D-CC). The board settles a 50% split of $522.51 on October 23. The letter states this was omitted from October 22 and November 2, 2018 letters (Trial Exs. J-12, J-13).

Days 367–373 — November 2–8, 2018

Counsel cites House Rules Section X.E and raises the demand to $7,566.08 (Trial Ex. J-13). The letter states 0.20 hours were billed on November 8 for contacting a Philadelphia Insurance adjuster, later contrasted with a November 6, 2020 verification in DC Dkt. 166.

Days 443–499 — January 17 and March 14, 2019

HIOSH inspects Elevator #1 and denies a regular permit for four items: inoperable emergency light, inoperable alarm bell, non-self-closing hoistway doors (ASME A17.1 Rule 110.3(a)), and a door gap greater than 3/8 inch (Trial Ex. D-S). A temporary permit issued March 14, 2019 expires April 30, 2019 (AOAO 0321, Trial Ex. D-T).

Proposed clauses

Clause A — amending HRS § 514B-154.5

(h) Before commencing any civil action, arbitration, or collection proceeding against a unit owner arising out of alleged damage to or use of any common-element elevator, boiler, or life-safety equipment regulated under chapter 397, the association or its managing agent shall provide to the unit owner, at no charge and without requiring an affidavit, at least thirty days prior to filing: (1) a copy of the unexpired state operating permit and most recent state inspection report in effect on the date of the incident; (2) the complete maintenance and repair contract governing the equipment; and (3) all contractor service tickets and records of substantially similar malfunctions during the preceding twenty-four months.

Clause B — amending HRS § 514B-157

(c) Notwithstanding subsections (a) and (b), an association shall not recover uncapped attorneys' fees under this section, and any fee award shall not exceed the twenty-five per cent assumpsit ceiling of section 607-14 (excluding any defense fees paid by an insurer without out-of-pocket payment obligation by the association), whenever: (1) the common-element equipment at issue was operating without a valid, unexpired state operating permit on the date of the incident; or (2) a claim initially demanded as reimbursement of a contractor repair invoice is recharacterized after the fact as a governing-document enforcement claim without a contemporaneous written violation notice.

Administrators

Cross-link HIOSH records and complaint intake with the permit-expiration registry; release post-April 30, 2019 files for HAW #67-127 and #67-120 under UIPA; apply HRS Chapter 397 and HAR Chapter 12-229. Tools: UIPA text.

Legislators

Review Clause A and Clause B for HRS Chapter 514B.

Media

Use the dockets and the inventory. Tools: eCourt instructions.

Insurers

Verify continuous state operating permits, disclose insurer-funded defense counsel, and do not treat insurer-paid defense (Claim #PHGG18101222417) as an out-of-pocket association loss.

Management companies

Audit posted permits, confirm full-service contract scope before owner billing, and answer HRS § 514B-154.5 requests. Checklist: cab page.

Directors

Under HRS § 514B-106(a), demand inspection certificates and vendor contracts before escalation, and use mediation under HRS § 514B-161.
